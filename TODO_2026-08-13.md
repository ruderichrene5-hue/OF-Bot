# Creating Instagram accounts — what we need, what we have

Written 2026-08-12 evening, after a day that proved the verification chain end
to end and, in doing so, built most of the parts a signup flow needs.

**Read §0 before writing code.** The build is straightforward; whether to run it
is the real question, and today produced the evidence to answer it.

---

## 0. The decision to make first

Two facts from today, both measured rather than argued:

**The existing fleet is the constraint, not the number of accounts.** Of 169
profiles, **60 are still flagged** after working ~45 of them. Today alone:
2 banned, ~16 logged out or with changed credentials, and — the one that
matters most — **two reactivated profiles refused to post** because the posting
loop could not prove which account was signed in:

    Not posting on ...:25384: could not prove it is signed in as @jasmindiecoolee.
    The clip stays queued -- posting it on the wrong account is the one outcome
    that cannot be undone.

So there are already accounts we own, are paying to warm up, and cannot post
from. Adding new ones does not fix that; it adds to it.

**Account creation is the most policed action on the platform**, and it breaks
Instagram's Terms of Use, so bans are a cost to price in rather than a risk to
engineer away. New accounts, made on cloud phones, verified with rented
numbers, is the exact signature they look for. Assume a meaningful share of
what we create is lost, at ~$0.60+ each plus six days of warm-up.

**Recommendation: do §8 first, or at least alongside.** A password reset on a
profile we already own is cheaper than a new account, needs the *same*
credential storage a signup flow needs, and the failure it fixes is one we have
already seen bite. If the answer to "how many of the 60 are recoverable?" is
"most", the signup flow is a much smaller job than it looks.

This is a business call, not an engineering one. The rest of this file assumes
it has been made.

## 2. What we do not have

### 2.1 Email accounts, and a way to read them — **the biggest gap**

There is **no mail client anywhere in the repo** (checked: no `imaplib`, no
IMAP, no inbox API; the `inbox` hits in `flows/instagram.py` are Instagram's
own UI strings). The `gmail` tag on 10 MLX profiles is a human doing this by
hand.

Signup needs email *or* phone. Email is the cheaper half:

- a catch-all domain, or one mailbox per account;
- an API or IMAP we can poll for the confirmation code;
- storage of address + password per profile.

Decide: **email or phone for signup verification.** Phone costs ~$0.60 a go and
today's German pool delivered roughly 1 in 2-3. Email is near-free once the
domain exists, and does not burn numbers that the *verification* flow also
needs.

### 2.2 The signup screen driver

New markers and handlers, in the shape of `verification.py`:

- the entry screen (`Create new account` / `Sign up`);
- name, username, password, date of birth;
- **username taken → Instagram's suggestion list.** This is the fiddly one:
  it must pick a name that satisfies §2.3's rules, not just tap a suggestion;
- email-or-phone choice, then the code screen (reuse the verification code
  handlers almost verbatim);
- "Save login info", "Turn on notifications", contacts sync — all onboarding
  screens `interruptions` may already clear.

**Nothing about these screens has been seen yet.** Every marker list today that
was written from general knowledge rather than a real dump was wrong in some
way. Assume the same here.

### 2.3 Identity data, with the naming rules baked in

`ONBOARDING_A_MODEL.md` documents constraints that are unforgiving because the
posting join key is `Profile Name.split()[0].lower()`:

- first word of the profile name = the model, and **one-word model names are
  strongly preferred**;
- **unique across the whole workspace** — two targets sharing a handle
  overwrite each other's spoofed variant file;
- must not contain the word `Link` — those are treated as link-in-bio accounts,
  not posting targets.

So the generator needs: first/last names, usernames satisfying the above *and*
free on Instagram, passwords, birthdays (adult, plausibly distributed), and a
uniqueness check against both MLX and Airtable before it commits.

### 2.4 Credential storage

Profiles (Cloning) has **no fields for a password or an email**. Needed:

- `IG Password`, `Signup Email`, `Email Password`, `Created At`;
- and a decision about storing passwords in Airtable at all — everyone with
  base access can read them. `/etc/adbbot/env` + a local store is the
  alternative, at the cost of the VAs not being able to log in by hand, which
  is exactly what today's `logged out` profiles need.

Per `adbbot-airtable-schema-writes`: on prod the **bot's token can create
fields** and the connector cannot, so adding these is a code task, not a
console one.

### 2.5 Profile picture and bio

The hand-off flow tracks these three (`Bio Done`, `Profile Picture Done`,
`First Post Done`) and nothing automates them. A created account is not a
usable account until they are done, and the first post must be **made by hand** —
an account whose first ever post is an automated reel is the one Instagram acts
on, which is the entire reason the hand-off gate exists.

So: **creation does not reduce the human work per account, it only moves it.**
Worth being explicit about before counting the flow as a win.

### 2.6 A signup runner

`verification_runner.py` is the template and most of it transfers: locks, the
per-account wall-clock budget, the cool-off, the fleet-level abort, the
dry-run-by-default. What changes is the *population* — it works a list of
blank MLX profiles rather than flagged ones — and the write-back.

## 3. Nothing on GitHub is worth taking

Searched 2026-08-12. The results split in two, and neither half helps:

- **Mature ADB projects that do not create accounts.**
  [GramAddict](https://github.com/GramAddict/bot) is the serious one —
  uiautomator2 + ADB, maintained, architecturally close to our `flows/`. It
  does engagement only. Same for
  [taktik-bot](https://github.com/masterFuf/taktik-bot).
- **Account-creation repos that are not ADB and not maintained** —
  [Insta-mass-account-creator](https://github.com/kaex/Insta-mass-account-creator),
  [Instagram-bulk-account-creator](https://github.com/Realjpp/Instagram-bulk-account-creator-),
  [instagram-account-creator](https://github.com/FrenziedIM/instagram-account-creator),
  [Ig-mass-account-creator](https://github.com/KING-OF-FLAME/Ig-mass-account-creator).
  These drive private APIs or web forms, which is a completely different
  surface from our device fleet and one Instagram breaks constantly. The last
  one says outright that OTP is still manual.

Running someone else's credential-handling code against our accounts is its own
risk, separate from whether it works. **Build it in our codebase.**

## 5. Build order

Nothing here is speculative work; each step produces evidence for the next.

1. **Decide §0** and §2.1 (email or phone).
2. **Watch a human create one account** on a cloud phone, with the recorder
   running. Every screen, in order, as a dump. This is the single highest-value
   hour in the whole plan — every marker list written without one has been
   wrong today.
3. **`flows/signup.py`** — markers + classification only, no actions. Replay
   the recording through it until every screen is named.
4. **The driver methods**, one screen at a time, `--apply` on one profile.
5. **Credential storage** (§2.4) before the first real run, or the first
   account created is an account we cannot log into.
6. **`signup_runner.py`** off the `verification_runner` template.
7. **One batch of 3**, measured: how many created, how many survive 24h, how
   many survive warm-up.

**Do not skip step 2.** Today's tally: the captcha screen classified as the SMS
code screen, "confirm you're human" matched a screen with nothing to answer,
and three separate marker lists written from general knowledge were wrong. The
only lists that worked first time were the ones written from a real dump.

## 7. Things today proved that apply directly

- **A screen labelled the *wrong* challenge is worse than one labelled `none`** —
  `none` stops the run; a wrong label makes the loop confidently do the wrong
  thing. Check every new marker list in both directions.
- **Never act on a screen you have not identified.** A downward swipe on the
  Android launcher opens the notification shade; a crop of the wrong image
  costs a paid solve and types a logo's name into a form.
- **Images render after the screen around them.** Blank at t=0 means look again
  in five seconds, not broken.
- **Positive evidence for success, always.** "No error visible" is not "it
  worked" — that mistake would have untagged a banned account today.
- **Free-by-default on anything with a price on screen.** The ads screen has a
  paid option next to the free one, and `interruptions` picks the free one by
  exact label.

## 8. The alternative, and probably the first job

**Find out how many of the 60 flagged profiles just need a password.**

Today's evidence says most of the fleet's problem is credentials, not
challenges: ~16 profiles are logged out or hand-tagged as such, two more could
not prove their identity at posting time, and only 4 of the first 25 worked
were solvable by verification at all.

A `flows/login.py` — sign in with a stored credential — would:

- reuse the same loop, driver, and runner as everything built today;
- need §2.4 (credential storage) and nothing else new;
- recover accounts that are already warmed up, already have bios and pictures,
  and already have queue rows waiting;
- and be measurable in a day.

If it works, it is strictly cheaper than creating replacements. If it does not,
we will know why, and the signup flow needs the same storage regardless.
